# Beejal — Homes vs Hotels — 3 minute pitch

**Before you stand up:** tool already open on screen, showing **London**. Scrolled to the top.
Second tab: the offline file, in case the wifi dies.

---

## 0:00–0:20 — The hook
*(Screen: top of the page. Do not touch anything. Look at the room, not the screen.)*

> "Somewhere in London tonight, a seven-year-old is doing her homework on a hotel bed, in a room
> her family has to leave in the morning.
>
> There are **one hundred thousand, nine hundred and thirty** children like her. The highest ever
> recorded — and not the number you've heard. Everyone still says eighty-five thousand.
>
> I'm Beejal. This is **Homes vs Hotels**."

---

## 0:20–0:55 — The money
*(Scroll to the red and green chart. Point at it while you talk.)*

> "It's also a budget story.
>
> Last year London boroughs spent **seven hundred and forty million pounds** on temporary
> accommodation. Three years ago, two hundred and forty three million. It **tripled**.
>
> Red is hotels. Green is prevention — what stops the next family ending up there.
> Red runs away. Green barely moves.
>
> Hotels now eat **seventy-one percent** of London's homelessness budget.
>
> And none of this is my estimate. It's what the councils reported to government."

---

## 0:55–1:15 — The waste
*(Stay where you are. No clicking. Let this land.)*

> "Now hold that next to this.
>
> In five years London approved **three hundred and twenty thousand** homes. It finished a
> hundred and eighty four thousand.
>
> **One hundred and twelve thousand were never even started.** Not delayed. Never begun."

---

## 1:15–1:50 — The twist  ← **this is the part that wins**
*(Scroll to the scatter plot.)*

> "Here's where I was wrong.
>
> I assumed the boroughs drowning in hotel costs would be the ones failing to build. I tested
> all thirty-one.
>
> **There is no relationship. None.**"

*(Point at one dot, then the other.)*

> "Barnet — worse than average pressure, and it still built more than it approved.
> Greenwich — less pressure, and thirteen thousand homes that never broke ground.
>
> This isn't lazy councils. It's worse, and more fixable.
> **London is building in the wrong places.**"

---

## 1:50–2:20 — Live demo
*(Ask the room. Wait for an answer. Click that borough on the map.)*

> "So I built this so anyone can check their own patch. Shout me a borough."

*(Click it. Wait for it to load. Then read straight off the screen.)*

> "Households. Children. How many were shipped outside the borough. What it costs."

*(Point at the email button — **do not click it**.)*

> "And a ready-written email to your councillor, with your own numbers already in it."

---

## 2:20–2:45 — The answer
*(Scroll to "So build here first".)*

> "And because the homes are in the wrong places, I can tell you where to start.
>
> Six boroughs, where need is worst **and** permission already exists.
> Tower Hamlets. Newham. Enfield. Southwark. Haringey. Wandsworth.
>
> Between them: **thirty-seven thousand approved homes never started**, twenty-four thousand
> families waiting.
>
> The permission to house all of them, one and a half times over, already exists. Nobody has to
> approve anything new."

---

## 2:45–3:00 — Honesty and close
*(Look up. Off the screen. Straight at the room.)*

> "Two things I could be wrong about. Unbuilt doesn't prove land banking — some is normal build
> time. And my correlation is a null result; thirty-one boroughs only catches a strong link.
>
> Every number is open government data. The data to argue for fixing this already existed.
> I just made it something you can type your own postcode into.
>
> Thank you."

# Cheat sheet

## Only five actions. Nothing else.

| Time | Do |
|---|---|
| 0:20 | Scroll to red/green chart. Point. |
| 1:15 | Scroll to scatter. Point at Barnet, then Greenwich. |
| 1:50 | Ask for a borough. Click it **on the map**. |
| 2:05 | Point at the email button. **Do not click.** |
| 2:20 | Scroll to "So build here first". |

## Six numbers, know them cold

- **100,930** children
- **£740m** a year, tripled in three years
- **71%** of the homelessness budget
- **112,104** homes never started
- **No link** between need and failure to build
- **6 boroughs, 37,000 homes** already approved

## If it goes wrong

**Wifi dies** — switch to the offline tab. Everything works except the postcode box.

**A borough shows a minus number** — that's real. Say: *"that one's actually delivering — it's
working through older permissions."*

**Tower Hamlets looks odd** — it recovers more than it spends on its own stock, so the page
deliberately hides the percentage rather than print nonsense. Say that; it makes you look careful.

**You lose your place** — jump to the six boroughs. That's the ending. Say it and stop.

**Running out of time** — cut the demo, never the six boroughs. The ending is what people vote on.

## The four questions you'll get

**"A null result isn't a finding."**
> "It is, when the whole room assumes the opposite. Everyone believes councils cry poverty while
> sitting on permissions. At borough level that isn't true — and it changes what you'd do about
> it. Target the pipeline instead of blaming councils."

**"Where do the cost numbers come from?"**
> "Form RO4 of the government's revenue outturn — what each council reported it actually spent.
> Net, not gross, because housing benefit covers most of the gross. Net is what lands on the
> borough's own budget."

**"Isn't the unbuilt gap just build time?"**
> "Partly, for the completions gap, and I say so on the page. But a hundred and twelve thousand
> homes never broke ground at all. Build time doesn't explain a site that never started."

**"Hillingdon at 287% of reserves — is it bankrupt?"**
> "No, and I don't claim it. It pays from revenue, not reserves. It shows how big the bill has
> got next to the money it could reach for in an emergency."
